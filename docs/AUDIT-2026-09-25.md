## Assessment

Vanguard has a coherent identity: inked OVA ships, cockpit radio, industrial
religion, a war maintained by a timetable, and a world economy that remembers
the player's actions. The Schedule is a particularly useful link between
fiction and gameplay. The Kessen challenge that premise through their ability
to build and act outside it; their strongest introduction is a consequential
encounter in the Graveyard.

The code is substantially beyond a visual prototype: a shared flight model,
seeded combat, replay checks, campaign runner, guild arcs, trade and ship
progression already exist. The next milestone should prove a polished playable
slice and a faithful trailer. Multiplayer and 200-frame Kessen Trains need
separate performance and state-coverage gates before becoming release promises.

Fresh 720p trailer samples show legible ship silhouettes, articulated gun
houses and a strong painted-space palette. Those samples used WebGL2 because
the existing capture launch flags failed to obtain WebGPU on this Windows
machine. The renderer explicitly disabled compute particles. They establish
composition, not full effects quality or native-GPU performance. No listening
review or full campaign playthrough was completed in this audit.

The trailer task subsequently found that full Chromium can enumerate the
Intel adapter but fails to create a GPU device (missing dxil.dll). Installed
Edge 153 (`channel: 'msedge'`) successfully creates devices with
`--use-angle=d3d11`, `--enable-unsafe-webgpu`, `--ignore-gpu-blocklist`;
the actual trailer is being verified with that configuration. Adapter
enumeration alone is insufficient. All 20 spoken trailer captions have
recorded clips, with three needing timing adjustments. The trailer task's
working edit adds one second for narration (90.384615 seconds total).
Final render verification remains its task.
