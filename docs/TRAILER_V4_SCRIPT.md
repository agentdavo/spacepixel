# Vanguard trailer — a story a new viewer can follow

Chief editorial rewrite, 25 September 2026. David finds V3 roughly acceptable
visually but less cinematic than intended, and rejects the script as largely
nonsensical. The new introduction must make sense to a twelve-year-old hearing
it once, without requiring knowledge of the lore. This supersedes V3's words
and its fixed timing. V3 remains preserved as a previous review cut.

## Replacement narration

Long ago, enormous gates in space let ships travel between distant worlds.

Then every gate failed.

Ships vanished. Worlds that traded with each other were suddenly cut off.

Four hundred years have passed. A few gates have been reopened.

They need a rare fuel from dying stars.

Two rival governments are fighting over that fuel.

Without that fuel, supply ships cannot get through the gates.

You join Vanguard, a squadron of fighter pilots.

Protect the ships under attack. Take on enemy warships. Bring your squadron home.

*[The battle and hull breakup play without narration. This direction is not spoken.]*

Now a signal is coming through one of the broken gates.

No one knows who is sending it.

But your ship's computer has answered.

*[Title: PROJECT VANGUARD — THE LONG DARK.]*

## Meaning and canon

The viewer learns what the gates do before hearing that they failed. Losing
travel cuts worlds off from one another. The reopened gates need scarce fuel;
the struggle to control it threatens shipping. The player joins a fighter
squadron and has understandable jobs. The final mystery concerns a specific
event involving the player's own ship.

Keep the speech adult and direct. The intended reading level is a clarity
standard, not a request for a children's voice or baby talk. Use natural
sentence stress and pauses, without stretching every word to sound grand.
Retain the replacement British female voice as the current review candidate.
Measure a fresh read before assigning final time windows; never force it into
the V3 caption schedule or speed it up to meet an old cut.

The lore review confirms the gate collapse, isolation, fuel dependence, rival
governments, broad campaign role and the broken-gate signal/computer response.
Sources: `LORE.md` premise and Lantern/Ebon-gas sections; `CAMPAIGN.md` Episode 5;
`src/game/campaign/missions.ts` Episode 5 computer reply. Four hundred years is
a spoken approximation of 431. Joining Vanguard is campaign scope, not a claim
that the player starts Episode 1 as a member.

Do not add the cause of the Shattering, the managed-war conspiracy, the signal's
sender or the campaign ending. Do not claim that a particular filmed convoy
carries food or medicine without evidence. No Kessen exposition is added.

The faction names and Ebon-gas name are deliberately omitted from speech.
They can be introduced later in the game, when the audience has a reason to
remember them. The countdown, survival/ascension slogans, ancient-ship riddle
and unspecified promise of reopening the galaxy are removed.

## Cinematic direction

Use the trailer task's timestamped review in `TRAILER_NEXT_SHOT_PLAN.md` as the
production proposal, with these chief decisions:

1. Establish a working travel route, then contrast it with a failed gate and
   wreckage. Show the purpose of the gates before dwelling on their size.
   These are illustrations of the history, not footage of the Shattering.
2. Introduce the player through a brief readable fighter action. Give the
   audience time to recognise the ship, target and result. Remove empty coast
   and any camera view obscured by the player's hull.
3. Establish the capital battle's geography, then follow fire, shield damage
   and hull failure in increasing simulation time. Use wide shots for scale,
   close shots for a visible action and the real HUD for understanding play.
   The 29-second repeated orbit and the 92-second HUD total are not targets.
4. Preserve the real continuous breakup, tightened to the useful lead-in and
   separated wreckage. Let the score withdraw before failure and leave room
   for the impact and aftermath. Never imply that a missile from the separate
   fighter encounter caused the capital ship's damage.
5. Retain a brief broken-gate ending because the rewritten hook now explains
   its relevance. Connect it to the player's ship and the computer response.
   This replaces the shot proposal's earlier recommendation to remove the
   gate ending entirely. Do not fabricate a gameplay computer-message overlay.

Aim near two minutes; the measured reading and dramatic beats determine the
length. The old 140–165-second requirement is superseded. Retain substantial
recognisable gameplay and clear uninterrupted HUD passages, but do not pad the
film to hit a HUD-seconds quota. Existing footage should supply most of the
edit. Up to three small native presentation pickups (working-route freighter,
battle wide, player flyby) are the proposed scope, not new game features.
