# THE KESSEN — race proposal

> *"Standing."*
> — Foreman Iolanthe Vey, Works Consist 9, the first word spoken on Kessendra,
> Year 0 (Reach reckoning), from inside a labour frame she had just stood up
> in the wreck

The name works on two levels. In the story it is short for **Kessendra**, the
junction they were sent to build and never reached. To the audience it is
**決戦**, *kessen*, "decisive battle". It sits against the Rot: the Schedule's
first rule is *No engagement shall be decisive.* The Kessen are the one power
in the Reach whose battles are.

## One-paragraph pitch

At the Shattering, a Timetable Authority works train was halfway through the
Anchorage Great Lantern. Its stern is still there, one of the forty wrecks in
the Timetable Graveyard. Its bow came out somewhere else: a heavy iron world in
a shoal of the Breath, where clocks run nine times fast and no fold-core can
run. The bow's holds carried no engines. They carried two thousand
neural-harness labour frames. The survivors lived in those frames, then farmed
and built in them, and after almost four thousand years of their own time they
became them. The two halves of the ship are still joined by a crack in space a
street wide, **the Seam**, which only walkers can use. Now the Breath is coming
back and the Graveyard is being cut up for scrap, so the Kessen have started
walking out of the dark.

## Origin: *The Appointed Hour*

- **TAW-9 *The Appointed Hour***, Timetable Authority Works Consist No. 9. A
  gate-works tender: 1,140 crew (fitters, platelayers, gangers and their
  families, since works postings lasted decades) and 2,300 **VARN** frames
  (*Vertical Assembly Rig, Neural-slaved*). These were labour exoframes that
  hung anchor scaffolding on Lantern rims. The neural harness let one fitter
  place a thirty-tonne ring segment to the millimetre.
- She was bound for **Kessendra Junction**, a new Lantern the Authority was
  building past Hesper. It was never finished; it may never have been started.
- **Year 0.** At the Shattering she was in the throat of the Anchorage Great
  Lantern. The stern (engines, fold-core, about 300 crew) stayed in the Reach
  and is one of the forty wrecks of the **Timetable Graveyard**. The bow
  (habitat, frame holds, 840 crew) went "somewhere that is not anywhere", and
  that somewhere had ground under it.
- **"Standing."** Foreman Iolanthe Vey climbed into a VARN frame in the
  flooded forward hold, stood it up and walked it out through the hull. Her
  first word, the frame's status call, is the Kessen greeting. It is their
  *"Lit."*

## Why mecha and not ships

The design has to answer this, so the answer comes in layers. Each one is
enough on its own; together they make a culture.

1. **Nothing else survived.** The bow held frames, not engines. The first
   shelter was a cockpit, the first plough was a thirty-tonne hand, and the
   first grave was dug by a frame.
2. **Ships cannot exist at home.** Kessendra sits in a **shoal** of the Breath.
   The lore already says that anything that folds space feels the Breath
   enormously. In the shoal a fold-core rings and shears the moment it is lit.
   No ship has ever lifted from Kessendra and none ever will. A body does not
   fold space.
3. **The only door is a street wide.** The Seam runs through the ship's broken
   spine and is about **14 m** across. Anything wider, or anything carrying a
   fold-core, shears in it. That sets a hard cap on frame size: three storeys
   and a crane boom.
4. **Carried means lost.** The ship carried them into nowhere. The founding
   trauma became a creed: *"I will not be carried."* To be a passenger is the
   worst thing a Kessen can be, and "passenger" is their worst insult. Even
   the dead walk home (see *The Standing Field*).
5. **The Breath is coming back, and they know it.** In the shoal the Breath is
   something you feel in your teeth. The Kessen have felt it deepen for
   centuries of their own time. They know what the crest will do to every
   fold-core in the Reach: *"When the Breath comes, the ships will ring. We will
   still be standing."* This is the secret they keep, and it is why they have
   come out now.
6. **Understanding, not maintenance.** The Reach runs on fossil tech it cannot
   open (*"we do not ask the engine why"*). The Kessen opened the VARN frames
   in Year 3 because they had to, and have been designing new ones ever since.
   They are the only people in the Reach who make new machines. That is why
   they hide, and why both governments would want them.

## Kessendra

| | |
|---|---|
| Type | Iron super-terran, 1.31 g, 26-hour day |
| Sky | **the Lid**: a permanent copper storm canopy, ionised by shoal shear. Lightning runs sideways along it. The sun has not been seen in 3,000 years. |
| Clock | **×9**. One Reach year is about nine on Kessendra. 431 years of the Long Dark is ~3,880 Kessen years. |
| Population | ~2.1 million, all on the Roster |
| Fold-cores | shear on ignition, everywhere in the system |
| Orbit | reached only by **the Stair** |

**Places.**

- **The Bow.** The forward half of *The Appointed Hour*, 4 km long,
  half-buried in a basalt plain. The first city and still the capital. The
  frame foundries are in its old holds, and the Seam's inner mouth, the
  **Spine Gate**, is in its broken spine.
- **The Standing Field.** The plain in front of the Bow. Every frame whose
  pilot has died stands here upright, facing the Spine Gate: about 190,000 of
  them. The oldest are rust shapes; the newest still have their paint.
  Children learn their letters from the stencils.
- **The Stair.** A 40 km electromagnetic track up the flank of Mount Vey, the
  only way to orbit. You don't ride the Stair, you *run* it: frames sprint up
  it on magnetic boots and are flung off the top.
- **The Roster Hall.** Every living Kessen is a name and a job on the Roster,
  and every frame a line on the same page.

**Why they are nine times older than we are.** The shoal's fast clock is why a
colony of 840 became a real *race* in what the Reach counts as four
centuries. They are shorter, denser and broad in the hand, grey-pale under the
Lid, and every one of them has a socket at the nape. It is also the price of
contact: a Kessen who spends a year in the Reach comes home to find nine years
gone.

## The Seam

A crack in space about 14 m wide, joining the Spine Gate on Kessendra to the
stern of *The Appointed Hour* in the Timetable Graveyard at Anchorage.
Crossing it is nine minutes of walking in the dark. Its walls ripple: this is
the one place a person can *see* the Breath. Anything wider than the Seam, or
anything with a fold-core, shears.

- It is the Kessen's only door, and it opens inside the Directorate's own
  fleet yard.
- The Graveyard Breakers (the existing Rustwake guild, see
  `src/game/guilds/guilds.ts`) are cutting up the hulks. Bone-Boss Magnus
  Ure "owns eleven hulks and wants a twelfth". **The twelfth is the stern of
  *The Appointed Hour*.** Cut it and the Seam closes forever.
- To cross the Reach's Lanterns without Ebon, Kessen **wake-walk**: a lit
  Lantern stays open about eleven seconds, and a frame drifting cold behind a
  convoy goes through with it. Reach sensors look for drive plumes and
  fold-cores, so a cold frame reads as a rock.

## The body: frames, Threading, Statures

- **Threading.** At seven a Kessen child gets a spinal socket and a first
  frame cut to their size. The **heartcase** (cockpit core) is fitted to one
  nervous system for life. **One pilot, one frame, never re-piloted.** Airframe
  0413 has had sixty-seven pilots; to a Kessen that is obscene.
- **The frame grows with the oath.** Rank is size. As a pilot earns rank the
  frame is rebuilt bigger around the same heartcase: *"The size of your body
  is the size of your promise."*
- **The Loom.** The neural mesh that joins the frames of a gang, a consist and
  a Train. Loomed pilots feel each other's footfalls. It makes **gang-fire**
  possible (below) and lets a gang walk a dead pilot's frame home.

**Two kinds of shield.**

- **Buffer**: a *held physics shield*, an inertial plate that eats momentum.
  It stops kinetic and explosive fire almost completely, and lasers burn
  through at 60 %. Board, kite and wall sizes.
- **Lid**: an *actual shield*, a projected energy dome named after the sky at
  home. It bleeds beams and harmonics, and it is momentum-gated: fast things
  bounce, slow things walk through. Bellows throw a 40 m dome; Anvils throw a
  wall 120 m across.
- **The key fact:** Reach capital shields are momentum-gated too. They stop
  what hits fast. **A frame that walks in at walking pace passes straight
  through**, and a ship can't slow down enough to do the same. That is the
  whole reason mecha beat ships at close quarters.

## One Train, one ship: why 200 can kill a capital ship

**Organisation** (works-train words): a **gang** of 8, a **consist** of 25 (1
Gantry, 2 Derricks, 5 Linesmen, 7 Shunters, 10 Fettlers), and a **Train** of 8
consists, which is **200 frames**. Proverb: *"A ship is killed by a Train."*

| Stature | Per consist | Per Train |
|---|---|---|
| V Gantry | 1 | 8 (e.g. 4 Anvil, 3 Piledriver, 1 Knell) |
| IV Derrick | 2 | 16 |
| III Linesman | 5 | 40 |
| II Shunter | 7 | 56 |
| I Fettler | 10 | 80 |

**Against BB-01 *Indomitable*** (hull 48,000; shield 9,000 in four 2,250
facings; regen 3 %/s after 8 s):

0. **Cold walk.** The Train approaches unpowered in a convoy's wake or a
   debris field, with no fold signature. The ship first sees it at knife range.
1. **Lid up (T+0).** The Anvils and Bellows throw Lid walls, and the battery
   and flak spend their first forty seconds on them. The Train loses about 10 %.
2. **The Hammer (T+30 s).** The Anvil raises its semaphore crest and the Loom
   fires ~100 guns within 40 ms onto one facing. Two volleys take down 2,250,
   and the Hammer keeps landing so the 8-second regen delay never runs out.
3. **Couple (T+60 s).** Shunters and Fettlers magnet-walk onto the hull
   *through* the dead facing. Mauls break turrets. Rivet teams go inside for
   the shield generator and the bridge (the batch-6 subsystem kill paths).
4. **Drive the spike (T+100 s).** Three Piledrivers × six drives × 1,800 × 1.5
   (explosive vs hull) = 48,600, which is more than 48,000. The keel breaks.
5. **Walk home (T+180 s).** The Loom walks the dead frames home. Nobody is
   carried.

Expected cost: 60–90 frames, 30–45 % of the Train. *"A ship costs a Train a
third of itself. That is the price, and we pay it."*

## Skeleton and animation

Every frame shares one **42-bone rig** (listed in
`scripts/concepts/kessen/mechkit.js`, `BONE_TREE`). Proportions change per
Stature, but the names don't, so every clip retargets to every frame.

- **Hierarchy:** root → hips → (4 skirt plates with spring secondary motion,
  thigh → shin → foot → toe ×2, spine → chest → neck → head → crest; hatch
  for the heartcase; shoulder → pauldron + upperarm → forearm → hand → fingers
  + thumb ×2; backpack → jet ×2; mount ×2 for shoulder weapons; weapon and
  shield sockets).
- **Rigid-part skinning:** each armour plate is bound 100 % to one bone. That
  is correct for plate mecha and cheap: 200 frames × 42 bones is 8,400
  matrices, one bone texture, and instanced parts per Stature.
- **Solved parts:** hydraulic pistons (thigh–shin, upperarm–forearm) are
  re-aimed after every pose; foot IK plants on terrain and on curved hulls
  (Couplings); upper-body aim IK is additive.
- **Clips:** idle (hydraulic sway), walk (heel-strike shake scaled by
  Stature), run, skate-dash, jump / land (knee compress, skirt bounce), space
  boost (legs trailing, jets vectored), turn-in-place, aim (additive),
  per-weapon fire and recoil, melee (maul swing, chisel slash, piledriver
  thrust), shield raise, Lid cast, couple (magnet-walk on a hull),
  directional hit-react, stagger, **kneel** (disabled, pilot alive) and
  **stand down** (pilot dead).
- **Kessen frames never fall over.** When a pilot dies the frame locks
  upright, grounds its weapon, bows its head and its visor goes dark. In space
  it drifts upright. This is the Standing Field pose, and it should be the
  race's signature death animation, with no ragdoll.

## Culture and language

- **Greetings.** "Standing." answered with "Stand." Farewell: "Walk on."
- **Death and injury.** A disabled frame has *knelt*; a dead pilot has *stood
  down*. The highest honour is *"She walked home."*
- **Insult.** "Passenger."
- **The Standing Field rite.** A dead pilot's gang walks the frame home over
  the Loom and stands it on the Field facing the Spine Gate. Nobody is
  carried, not even the dead.
- **Running late.** The Kessen kept the Timetable Authority's clock. Every
  heartcase has a line stencilled on it: *WORKS CONSIST 9 — RUNNING LATE*.
  They know exactly how late they are: 3,880 years, by their count. (This
  pays off with 0413; see hooks.)
- **The Hammer-song.** The Choir sings the Hymn and the Rustwake sing
  haul-songs. The Kessen **hammer**: a call-and-response work song timed to
  footfalls and gang-fire, sung on open bands in battle.
- **Government.** The **Roster** (every name and job) is kept by the **Chief
  Ganger**, who is elected by the **Muster** of Foremen. Only Foremen pilot
  Gantries.

### People

- **Iolanthe Vey** (Year 0). Works foreman of Consist 9. She stood up the
  first frame and said "Standing." Her VARN is the first frame on the Standing
  Field.
- **Chief Ganger Ruth Aske.** Keeper of the Roster, old even by Kessen
  reckoning. She sent her own grandson through the Seam.
- **Linesman Corin Aske, "Nine"** (Plumb). Envoy and scout in the Reach for
  eleven months. His daughter was two when he left and is ten now. A possible
  ally or wingman for Vanguard.
- **Foreman Hesketh Dray, "Anvil"** (Anvil). Commands the 4th Train. A hawk
  who wants to hit the Graveyard Breakers before they reach the stern.
- **Fettler Wynn** (Tongs), age eleven. A child fitter who fixes a Kestrel
  in the dark and leaves a stencil on it.

### The other powers

- **Terran Directorate.** Since 398 the Office of Continuity has logged eleven
  unexplained losses in the Graveyard, all filed as "structural accidents". It
  has a file called UNSCHEDULED. Pryce would want the Kessen as a lever.
- **Zenith Hegemony.** Choir crystal rings at the Knell. The Choir calls the
  Kessen *the Tuneless* and fears them in the way a bell fears a hammer.
- **Rustwake.** The only people who have traded with them. Kessen fix
  haulers in the dark and take payment in seeds and archives, never grams.
  The clan legend of the tin ghosts who mend your drive while you sleep is
  true. *"Nothing in the black is ever truly lost."*
- **The Builders.** The Kessen don't know the Builders' names, but they know
  the Breath by feel. They are a witness to the campaign's secret history, not
  a key to it.

## Campaign hooks (proposals, not rewrites)

1. **"Graveyard Shift".** A Breakers contract at Anchorage. Ure's cutters
   reach a stern whose stencil reads *WORKS CONSIST 9*, and something walks
   out of it.
2. **"Running Late".** 0413's FCS reads a heartcase's Timetable ID and answers
   in dialect: *WORKS CONSIST 9 — YOU ARE RUNNING LATE. SERVICE WILL RESUME
   SHORTLY.* The Kessen have never heard a machine speak their clock.
3. **The Seam question.** Protect the stern and keep the door open, or let
   Continuity seize it (the Directorate's lever). This is a choice with teeth
   inside the existing guild system.
4. **At the crest (Ep 19).** When the Breath breaks over the Reach and every
   fold-core rings, a Train stands between the Nexus and whatever comes
   through. They don't ring. *"We told you we'd still be standing."*

## Implementation notes (for when it moves to code)

- `FactionId` gains `kessen`; add a livery to `Factions.ts`.
- There is no `SkinnedMesh` use in `src/` yet. Frames want a **FrameKit**
  (like `HullKit` / `ShipBuilder`) that builds rigid parts on bones, plus an
  instanced bone-palette path for 200-frame Trains under WebGPU.
- Sim: a walker locomotion model (ground and zero-g), **Couplings**
  (magnet-walk on a capital hull, foot IK on the hull grid), gang-fire timing
  on the Loom, and boarding as a subsystem damage path in `Damage.ts`.
- Audio (voices / score session): a Kessen score (steel percussion, anvil,
  call-and-response work song) and barks ("Standing.", "Lid up!", "Drive the
  spike!", "She walked home.").
