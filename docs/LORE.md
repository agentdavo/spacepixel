# PROJECT VANGUARD — Series Bible

> *"Service to Meridian, Hesper, Tessaly and all points coreward is delayed.
> We apologise for the inconvenience. Service will resume shortly."*
> — golden-age timetable beacon, Anchorage Great Lantern, broadcasting on a loop
> for 431 years

## Premise

**Year 431 of the Long Dark. The Meridian Reach.**

Once, the stars were joined. A network of ancient gates — the **Lanterns** —
tied the galaxy together, and for twenty-two centuries humanity rode it on a
published timetable. Then, in a single day called **the Shattering**, every
Lantern went dark at once. The ships in their throats were never seen again.
The colonies were left where they stood, alone, in the dark.

Four centuries later, six Lanterns burn again in the Reach, lit by a volatile
black-light isotope called **Ebon-gas** that can only be skimmed from dying
stars. Everything that flies is a fossil: golden-age hulls, engines and
flight computers copied, patched and prayed over by people who can keep them
running but cannot say why they work. Two powers fight a slow war over the
gas. The **Terran Directorate** counts every gram and calls it survival. The
**Zenith Hegemony** sings to its ships and calls it ascension. Neither is
winning. That, it turns out, is the point.

And at the edge of the Reach, beyond a gate that leads nowhere, something is
counting down the prime numbers, one a day.

You fly **Vanguard** — the 13th Independent Squadron — the unit that goes
through first.

## Tone

1990s OVA space opera played straight: Macross's cockpit intimacy, Wing
Commander's ready-room family, Legend of the Galactic Heroes' politics seen
from below, Gunbuster's leaps of scale and time. Bold ink lines, painted skies,
beam spam and missile circuses — and then silence, when the thing on the
screen is the size of a moon.

The register moves between three keys and the writing should know which one
it is in:

- **The cockpit.** Terse, funny, frightened, specific. Ozone and old coffee.
  People who love each other and will not say so on an open channel.
- **The machine.** Liturgy and maintenance. Counted litanies, sealed cores,
  a flight computer that speaks a dead timetable dialect. Reverence without
  understanding, and the cost of that.
- **The sublime.** Clarke, not Lucas. Scale, patience, restraint. When the
  Builders speak, nobody makes a joke for eleven seconds.

**Themes.** Maintenance versus understanding. The cost of counting people as
grams. Two faiths that each preserved half of a truth. Grief carried forward
as duty ("keep the light"). A war that was never meant to end, ended by
people who refused their assigned numbers. And an ending that is not a
victory but an invitation: the gates hum, the horizon opens, and humanity is
asked — not told — to go.

## A history of the Long Dark

### Deep time: the Builders and the Breath

Eleven million years ago a species we will never see watched a wave move
through the galaxy. It came outward from the core like a ripple across a
pond: a periodic compression of spacetime they called **the Breath**. Planets
do not feel it. Stars barely do. Anything that folds space feels it
enormously. The Builders built their road — the Lanterns — to ride it: a gate
in true phase drinks the Breath the way a sail drinks wind. A gate out of
phase is a bell struck at the wrong note. It rings, then cracks.

Each Breath was a little deeper than the last. So the Builders tuned their
road, anchored it with moon-sized spheres that hold local space steady, left
keys to re-tune it and a clock to say when, and went outward past the rim of
the disc where the Breath does not reach. They did not ascend. They moved.
They left the road lit behind them for whoever came next.

### The Timetable Era (c. 2,200 years)

Humanity came next. From the **Cradle** — the world the old records call
Earth — it found the Lanterns already humming and did what humanity does with
a gift it does not understand: it wrote a schedule. The **Meridian Concord**
and its **Timetable Authority** ran the network to the minute. Children took
the gate to school. The Concord built the ship patterns that still fly today —
the VF-27 Kestrel among them — and eleven **Clavis-class gate tenders** that
went out every 431 years, at the crest of the Breath, to keep the Lanterns in
phase. Most people thought the tuning was a ceremony.

In the budget year 2,197 the Authority laid up ten of the eleven tenders as a
saving. The gates, it was agreed, held their own phase now.

### The Shattering (Year 0)

The next crest came eleven months later. The last tender, *The Long
Patience*, tuned Corouhold and did not reach Anchorage. Across the galaxy, the
untuned Lanterns rang and cracked. Everything in transit went somewhere that
is not anywhere. At Anchorage alone, forty ships were caught halfway through
the Great Lantern; their wrecks — the **Timetable Graveyard** — still hang
around the dead ring, and a beacon there still promises that service will
resume shortly.

No one in the Reach knows any of this. The Directorate teaches the Shattering
as an accident. The Hegemony teaches it as a judgment. Both are wrong, and
the Hegemony is closer.

### The Long Dark (0–212 AS)

Six inhabited systems in the Reach, suddenly alone. Anchorage had shipyards
and no ore; Meridian had ore and nine million mouths; Hesper had a Lantern that
still hummed and a nobility who decided the humming was a voice; Tessaly had a
dying red giant nobody wanted, yet. At Anchorage, the **Candle Years** — thirty-
one winters of failing reactors — produced the **Order of the Keeping**,
founded by Hollis Marrow, a Timetable controller who kept four reactors alive
by ritual because ritual was the only thing she had that worked.

### The Relighting (212 AS)

A Rustwake prospector named Absalom Tey skimmed Tessaly's corona and came home
with a hold of something that ate the light: Ebon-gas. Poured into the feed of
the dead Anchorage–Meridian Lantern, it opened the ring for eleven seconds. The
pilot's first word from Meridian space — *"Lit."* — is still the Keeping's
highest greeting. Within fifty years six Lanterns burned, narrow Ebon-fed
bridges over a dark that went on forever on every side. They are relit, not
repaired: out of phase, fed by a crude substitute, and nobody knows it.

### The Long Attrition (394 AS – present)

The **Accord of Six Lights** bound the Reach into one market for ninety-one
years. In 394 the Hegemony annexed the Tessaly Ebon-fields; the Directorate
mobilised; seven years of real war produced the **Treaty Line** and the
**Observance**, the weekly ritual in which both sides fly the Line together,
guns cold. Since 403 the war has been pickets, raids and convoy actions —
neither side able to afford victory, neither able to afford peace while the
other holds half the supply chain.

Or so the history says. Since 398 the war has been run from a joint
spreadsheet. See *The Rot*, below.

## The machine: fossil technology

Nothing that flies in the Reach was designed in the Reach. Every hull pattern,
drive core and flight computer is a golden-age original or a copy made by
people who can reproduce the *shape* of a thing perfectly and the *reason* for
it not at all. The Directorate calls this heritage engineering. Pilots call it
fossil tech. Wardens call it the Keeping.

It produces a particular culture. A civilisation that cannot make anything new
learns to love maintenance. Reverence replaces curiosity; repair replaces
design; a machine kept running for three hundred years becomes holy in the most
literal sense. Directorate sensor suites can resolve a coin at a light-second
and display it only in green. Choir crystal is grown in foundry-gardens that
work only when sung to. Nobody asks what else the machines could do.

### The Order of the Keeping

The **engine-wardens** wear brown, shave their heads to keep hair out of the
intakes, and carry a torque-key on a cord where a priest might carry a cross.
They serve in the fleet as mechanics and, rarely, by dispensation, as pilots
("riding their vows"). Their rule: what works is kept; what is kept is not
opened; what is not opened is not questioned. *A sealed heart is a holy
heart.* They will rebuild a golden-age drive eleven times around its core and
never once unseal the core itself.

Their text is **the Seven Keepings**, counted on the fingers before any engine
is lit:

> *First keeping: the seal holds.*
> *Second keeping: the feed runs clean.*
> *Third keeping: the fire is fed and not starved.*
> *Fourth keeping: the cold is let out.*
> *Fifth keeping: the old words are said.*
> *Sixth keeping: we do not ask the engine why.*
> *Seventh keeping: we thank it, and we go.*

The litany is the campaign's spine. It is read in full in Episode 1, it times
the defence of a stalled barge in Episode 2, it is broken in Episode 18 when
Warden Oduya opens the *Long Patience*'s archive, and in Episode 20 it gains a
line: *Eighth keeping: we ask it why. And we listen.*

### Airframe 0413

The player's Kestrel. Its flight computer core is a sealed golden-age original,
600+ years old; everything else has been replaced — the fuselage eleven times,
the starboard canard forty-one. It has had sixty-seven pilots; fourteen did not
come home, and the core was recovered and reinstalled every time. It will not
accept a pilot name; it displays POINT. It hums near Lanterns. It occasionally
answers in the Timetable dialect (*SERVICE RESUMES SHORTLY*). In Episode 5 it
answers the Signal. In Episode 13 the Monolith uses it as a dictionary. In
Episode 16, inside the Nexus corridor, it speaks in the first person for the
first time: *I REMEMBER ALL OF THEM, POINT. SIXTY-SEVEN. I KEPT THEM.* The
machine is the myth's witness; that is what "The Myth and the Machine" means.

### The Lanterns

Seamless black rings four to nineteen kilometres across. Lit, a Lantern holds a
sheet of light across its throat like a still lake seen from underneath;
transit is four to nine subjective seconds that most pilots will not talk
about. Six burn in the Reach. Scores more hang dark. The **Null Lantern** is
the last before the void, and the Signal comes through it. (Mechanically, the
Null system's *lit* gate is the lane from Halaedon; the Null Lantern of the
story is a second, dead ring facing outward.)

## Ebon-gas

A metastable exotic phase condensed from the shed envelopes of dying stars:
Tessaly's red giant, the Rustwake Ember, and a third source the Board will not
name. Stored under magnetic confinement; measured in grams. One gram carries a
fighter through a Lantern; forty kilograms carries a carrier.

You do not see Ebon. You see what it does to everything else: crack a flask in
a dark hold and the dark gets darker and the edges of things start to glow
violet, as if lit from somewhere outside the room. **Black light.** Deck plating
seems to stand half an inch in front of itself. Your hands look like someone
else's. It tastes of pennies from across the room. Everyone stops talking.

Ebon is the Reach's spice. Every allocation, every ration card, every raid and
every alliance is a dispute over its flow. The Directorate controls refining
(the Tey works at Castellan); the Hegemony controls Tessaly's fields; the
Rustwake clans skim the dregs and sell to everyone. The truth the campaign
uncovers is that Ebon is a crude substitute: an aligned Lantern needs no fuel.
It drinks the Breath.

## The two heavens

### The Terran Directorate (faction id `concord`)

*"Keep the light."*

Eleven million people across Meridian, Anchorage and their habitats, governed
from the **Counting House** on Castellan's inner ring by a Board nobody
nominated. The charter uses "survival" nineteen times and "freedom" never. Its
virtues are real: nobody starves, every child is schooled, every reactor is
kept. Its vice is those virtues seen from the other end — a state that measures
everything eventually believes what it cannot measure does not exist. It calls
itself Terran after a Cradle none of its citizens has seen; if we are Earth's
heirs, the golden age is ours, and so is everything it left.

- **The Board of Allocation** decides who eats, flies and dies, in grams. Its
  chair, **Corwin Pryce**, never says "death"; he says "expenditure".
- **The Office of Continuity** — intelligence and audit.
- **The fleet** still calls itself the *Meridian Concord Defense Force* (MCDF)
  after the golden-age government. Nobody has had the allocation to repaint.
- **Look:** ivory hulls, cobalt panels, signal-orange ID bands; model-kit
  mechanical design. Every ship is a fossil pattern rebuilt at Anchorage.
- **Key ships:** VF-27 Kestrel (interceptor), VF-31 Harrier (strike), SB-9
  Warhorse (bomber), FFC-12 Lantern Guard (picket corvette), CVS-07 *Hesperus
  Dawn* (carrier), BB-01 *Indomitable* (last dreadnought in the Reach).
- **The Bastion:** the home carrier fleet at Anchorage — *Hesperus Dawn*,
  *Lodestar*, *Constant*, eleven escorts. Destroyed in Episode 10.

### The Zenith Hegemony (faction id `choir`)

*"Be witnessed." / "Ascend."*

The Hegemony believes the golden age was a fall: humanity was handed the stars
before it was grown enough to hold them, and the Shattering was a child
dropping a lamp. The answer is not to relight the lamp but to grow up —
**Ascension** — pursued with a religious order's discipline and a stud-book's
pedigree-keeping. The **Ascendant Houses** of Hesper (nineteen once, eleven now)
elect **the Zenith** for life; the title is also a direction, the point
directly overhead.

- **The Choir** is the Hegemony's armed service: pilots are **Cantors**,
  squadrons **Measures**, dreadnoughts **Cathedrals**. Every transmission opens
  with the **Intonation** (four rising tones, frequency-exact, sung by the drive
  crystals). In combat they sing the **Hymn of Ascent** on open bands — not as
  terror, though it works as that, but because a Cantor that stops singing
  flies worse. Nobody knows why. (The Hymn's intervals are prime ratios. It is
  a golden-age tuning procedure preserved as liturgy. It is half of the key.)
- **The Treasury** under Hierarch-Treasurer **Varro Quillon** runs the
  Tessaly fields and prays beautifully while counting.
- **Look:** violet-black crystalline hulls, magenta eyes and seams,
  forward-swept blades, cathedral silhouettes with resonance spires.
- **Key ships:** SC-4 Cantor, SC-7 Psalter (torpedo bomber), SCE-2 Vesper
  (escort / "cruiser" / spire-tender), SCD-1 Cathedral (the *Magnificat*, the
  Zenith's throne-ship *Altitude*).

### The Rot

Since 398 AS the war has been a thermostat. Allocator-General Pryce and
Hierarch-Treasurer Quillon co-sign a quarterly **Schedule of Engagements**:
where each battle will be fought, how many fighters each side will "expend",
how much Ebon each loss releases to market, and the agreed price floor (88
shares a gram). *No engagement shall be decisive.* The war keeps both
populations frightened and governable, both treasuries full, and — the Zenith
tolerates it for this — humanity looking at its feet instead of the stars.

The Schedule surfaces in Episode 8 ("Engagement 114: expected expenditure,
eleven fighters. 13th Squadron requested by name as *costly and visible*").
The Fall of the Bastion is Engagement 131. It is read aloud to the whole
Directorate in Episode 18.

### The Rustwake Clans (faction id `rustwake`)

*"Nothing in the black is ever truly lost."*

Thirty-odd clans skimming the Rustwake Ember's last breath from hauler-stations
in the Belt. Officially neutral: everyone buys from them and nobody protects
them. Ships built from both sides' wrecks, keeping both sides' paint
(Scrapjacks). They vote by shouting, settle debts in grams, and sing
haul-songs — the only untaxed music in the Reach. They call themselves the
only honest people in the Reach, since they are the only ones who admit it is
about the gas.

## The Signal, the Builders and the Breath

*(Writers' eyes only: this is the campaign's secret history.)*

- **The Signal.** From 430 AS, pickets at the Null log bursts of identical
  pulses from the void. Every burst's pulse count is prime; each is the next
  prime down; bursts arrive every 25 h 51 min. It began at 1,999. The
  player first hears it at 1,009; Kestrel 0413 answers it with one pulse
  (*ACKNOWLEDGE RECEIPT*). Both governments suppress it — the first thing they
  have agreed on in 37 years.
- **The Monolith** (the Builders' *anchor*): a sphere 3,470 km across beyond
  the Dead Zone, eleven million years old, colder than space, where clocks run
  slow and the Caul's lightning stops twenty kilometres out as if at a wall.
  The Signal is the Monolith itself, counting. It is the Builders' clock.
- **The Oracle** (the Anchor Archive) speaks to anyone who brings a
  golden-age core close enough to act as a dictionary. It never answered the
  Choir's six years of hymns because the Choir brought only one kind of song.
- **The Nexus**: a Lantern two hundred kilometres across, whole, unlit, its rim
  holding the map of every road in the galaxy. Whoever holds it can open
  every gate at once or seal every gate forever.
- **The Clavis**: *The Long Patience* is not carrying a key; she *is* one. A
  golden-age instrument — twelve sealed nodes along a resonator spine — played
  with the Hymn of Ascent and kept in running order by the Keepings. The
  Directorate wants her as a sword (the **Gladius Program**: open every
  Hegemony gate before the crest). The Zenith wants the Nexus as a seal. Only
  the Builders' intended use tunes the road.
- **The Zenith's motive.** Ottavian read the Builders' fragment at Hesper as a
  young scholar. He knows about the Breath. He knows the golden age broke the
  road through thrift and pride. He believes humanity will do it again, and
  that the only mercy is to seal every Lantern cleanly at the crest: every world
  an Earth, every Earth a cradle — *and a cradle is a safe place to be a child
  forever.* He is not a villain. He is a grandfather who has decided nobody,
  including his own granddaughter, is worthy of the stars.

## The cast

**Vanguard, 13th Independent Squadron.** Tradition: *Vanguard goes through
first. The Point flies One. The skipper flies Two, behind the Point, where she
can see everyone's mistakes. When someone does not come back, their mug stays
on the hook.*

- **Vanguard 1, "the Point"** (the player). Silent. Ferry and picket pilot of
  airframe 0413 in Chapter I, then Point. Others finish their sentences.
- **Lt. Cmdr. Saoirse Kade, "Abbess"** (Vanguard 2). 41, Cloister orphan,
  nineteen years in the cockpit. Dry as a checklist, kind only in actions.
  *Arc:* loyal to a Directorate she despises → released to her own judgment by
  a dying captain → wants to burn the Counting House down → chooses to hold the
  Ring instead, and speaks to the Zenith, engine-orphan to scholar-king. She
  owns the campaign's first and last lines about the rim.
- **Lt. Teodor Castellanos, "Jackpot"** (Vanguard 3). Runs the kill pool and the
  jokes; collects golden-age junk; names the Monolith after a film he never
  saw. Asks in Episode 6 what is past the rim. Dies at the Nexus covering Salt,
  laughing, leaving the pool to the only pilot who never cheated.
- **Warden-Brother Ilesanmi Oduya, "Candle"** (Vanguard 4). Engine-warden
  riding his vows. Blesses engines, not people. Stays alone aboard the ghost
  ship to wake her. *Arc:* the Sixth Keeping kept → broken → rewritten. Adds
  the Eighth Keeping.
- **Ensign Wren Talbot, "Sparrow"** (Vanguard 5). Nineteen, idealist, quotes
  golden-age poetry at bad moments. *Arc:* belief in the Directorate → its
  collapse → a harder belief in the Oracle's "tune the road." Sees Ada's chart
  in the sky.
- **Lt. Soren Achterberg, "Salt"** (Vanguard 6). Ex-Ebon convoy escort; knows
  what a gram costs. Takes the *escape* side of the schism and leaves with the
  clans. Comes back at the Nexus. Never says goodbye; that is how you know.

**The Bastion.**

- **Captain Augustin Oyelaran**, CVS-07 *Hesperus Dawn*. Grandfather to his
  crew and to **Ada**, seven, whose crayon star-chart hangs on the CAG board.
  Holds the hangar doors open while the Dawn dies. Last words: *"Tell her the
  lines go somewhere … Go where the light is. Keep the light."*
- **Lt. (jg) Hana Okafor**, Dawn Control. "Deck is green," to the end.
- **Captain Mireya Rook**, BB-01 *Indomitable*. Obeys every order to the letter,
  which is how she finds the one she will not obey. Relieves Pryce: *"Put it
  on my ration card, Allocator."*

**The Directorate.**

- **Commander Yevgenia Aubrac, "Ledger"**, Office of Continuity. The auditor
  who signed off on the Schedule for nine years without letting herself know
  what the numbers were. Decrypts the Canticle, leaks the Rot, reads it aloud
  on the Allocation Hour with her own name in it.
- **Allocator-General Corwin Pryce.** Reasonable. Always reasonable. Sells the
  Bastion; wants the Gladius. *"You think it's a key. It's a lever."*

**Rustwake.**

- **Temperance "Tem" Marsh, "Magpie"**, hauler *Magpie's Due*. Eye lost to a
  cracked Ebon flask at nine. Charges the Directorate triple, the Hegemony
  double and friends nothing, which is how you find out whether you are one.

**The Hegemony.**

- **Dame-Cantor Isaura Sarre-Aurelian, "Psalm"**, First Cantor of Hesper,
  granddaughter of the Zenith. A true believer: shoots her own wingman's
  weapons off for breaking the Observance and apologises across the Line.
  *Arc:* rival → asks why the Anchor speaks to the unascended → hears her
  grandfather's plan ("Even us?" "Especially us.") → comes aboard the
  *Patience* weapons cold → sings the tuning in the right key.
- **Ottavian Sarre-Aurelian, the Nineteenth Zenith, His Serene Altitude.**
  Scholar-king, tender and certain. Keeps a golden-age child's rattle on his
  desk. At the crest, hearing his granddaughter sing, does not fire his seal.
  Takes the *Altitude* outward through the Hesper Lantern, alone. His only
  message: *"Tell the pilot I was wrong about the children."*
- **Hierarch-Treasurer Varro Quillon.** Pryce's mirror. Escapes.

**Voices without faces.**

- **FCS** — airframe 0413's flight computer (see above).
- **The Oracle** — the Anchor Archive. Calm, plural, translated. *"We are not
  gone. We are ahead."*

## Writers' notes

- **Radio lines ≤ 140 characters.** One thought per line. Let silence work;
  use `delay` rather than filler.
- **Nobody says "last flight," "easy one" or "what could go wrong."** Kade
  catches herself in Episode 19.
- **The FCS speaks in ALL CAPS** and never uses "I" until Episode 16.
- **The Oracle speaks in ALL CAPS until translation passes ~80 %,** then in
  plain sentences.
- **Choir liturgy:** "Be witnessed" (greeting / acknowledgement / threat),
  "Ascend" (farewell), "the Altitude" (the Zenith), "unwitnessed" (disgraced).
  Hymn lines are prefixed `(sung)`.
- **Grams, not credits.** Directorate characters price things in grams and
  shares; Rustwake in grams and favours; the Choir pretends not to price at all.
- **Restraint in the finale.** Episode 20 has no enemies, no music cue the
  script can call, and a debrief of seven words.

## Reconciliation with the code

- Faction ids are stable: `concord` = Terran Directorate (the fleet keeps the
  fossil name *Meridian Concord Defense Force*); `choir` = Zenith Hegemony (the
  Choir is its armed service); `rustwake` = the Rustwake clans; `unknown` = the
  Builders and their artefacts. `src/assets/Factions.ts` names and mottoes are
  in-world compatible (the MCDF and "Keep the light" survive as fossils; the
  Choir's "Every gate is a throat…" can be read as an older Choir saying).
- The old "Cartographers" are the golden age's name for the **Builders**.
- The **Lanterns** are the Builders' gates, relit with Ebon.
- Campaign data lives in `src/game/campaign/`; the episode outline is in
  [`docs/CAMPAIGN.md`](CAMPAIGN.md).
